Ask ten walkers what makes a trail good and you will get ten answers: the view, the summit, the swim at the end, the quiet. Distance is what trail signs print, so it is what most of us compare.

This page puts 57 trails from six national parks side by side, from a 400 metre stroll to the 12 sections of the Larapinta Trail. It asks a simple question: what do the numbers say about the effort a trail asks of you?

TrailA walk with its own published distance and time. Larapinta is split into its 12 official sections.
GradeThe park agency's difficulty rating. Most use grades 1 (easiest) to 5. I group them into four bands.
Summit routeA way up a peak with no formed track. Queensland Parks calls Mt Barney's routes "not walking tracks".

How colour is used

Five clearly different hues, one per area, kept the same on every chart. Areas are also named by a label or legend, so colour is never the only clue.

Grade
  • Easy
  • Moderate
  • Hard
  • Very hard
  • Not graded

Green to dark red, like a traffic light: the warmer and darker, the harder the trail. Grey means the source gives no grade.

Time
  • Under 4 hours
  • 4 hours or more
  • Longest on average

Teal is the short side and orange the long side, and a darker orange means a longer time. Orange is also the trail-marker colour of this page.

Grey on maps
  • No trail data

Land with no trail in this story is grey. A state with data is filled with a colour instead.

Six parks, five states and territories

Mt Barney, Springbrook, Wilsons Promontory, the Flinders Ranges, the Stirling Range and the Larapinta Trail sit a long way apart.

On the maps, trails inside the same area are drawn as one symbol so the maps stay readable. Every trail is still listed separately in the charts below.

Kilometres are only half the story

Two trails of the same length can ask for very different days. Time shows what the terrain is really like.

Hard means different things

Grades, summits and steep sections each add difficulty that distance hides.

Disclaimer

Trail times on this page are walking times only. Some trails may actually take days to finish, because the times used here do not include breaks, rest stops, meals, overnight stays and so on. All figures (distances, times and grades) are just what the credible source for each trail says; I have not measured or checked them myself.
